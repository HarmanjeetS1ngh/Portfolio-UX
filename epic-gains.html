02 — Concept

Why a virtual pet?

Points only reward the past. A virtual pet that reacts to your daily actions builds a habit you don't want to break.

Virtual pets make us care. Most fitness apps just show numbers. But in Epic Gains, if Arjun skips his workout, his pet gets sad. If he works out, his pet levels up and evolves. The goal isn't just to get fit—it's to make sure you don't let your buddy down.

03 — Design

Bringing the Idea to Life.

We focused on the user's story first.
01

Make starting easy

A quick 5-step quiz creates a custom workout plan instantly. You pick your pet at the very end of the quiz. This makes you feel invested before the real work begins.

Design Decision

Companion selection is step 5, not step 1 — the quiz builds investment before the hook is introduced.

02

Show growth, not just stats

Your daily plan, progress, and pet's mood are the first things you see. Earning points to level up your pet changes your goal from "getting fit" to "not skipping tomorrow."

Design Decision

Companion XP and today's workout share equal visual weight — progress and plan feel like one thing.

03

Keep workouts simple

You see one exercise at a time. Built-in videos show you how to do it. The app tracks your sets automatically so you don't have to stop. You earn points during your workout, keeping you motivated in the moment.

Design Decision

Auto-logging removes mid-workout friction. Default flow assumes completion; undo handles edge cases.

UX Trade-off

Auto-log can't distinguish a completed set from a mid-set rest. Haptic confirmation is earmarked for v2.

04

Celebrate every win

When you finish a workout, your pet celebrates with you. Seeing your pet happy is the best reward.

Design Decision

Pokémon celebration precedes the summary. Emotional payoff first, data second.

05

Friendly reminders, no guilt

If you miss a day, the app doesn't make you feel bad. Instead, it sends a friendly message like:

"Hey… are we still training today? Your pet is waiting for you! 💪"
Design Decision

Re-engagement uses the companion's voice, not the app's. Emotional nudges, not pressure.

04 — Delivery

One Simple Solution.

We didn't build three different features. We used one main idea—the virtual pet—to solve fear, confusion, and quitting.
Fear ↓ Guidance

A quick quiz gives you a clear plan.

Confusion ↓ Simplicity

One exercise at a time, with built-in video guides.

Quitting ↓ Accountability

A pet that needs you keeps you coming back.

05 — Reflection

What I Learned in 28 Hours.

This fast project taught me a lot about making quick design choices and planning for the future.
01

Less time meant better focus

Having only 28 hours forced me to focus on the main idea first. When the core idea is strong, designing the screens is easy.

02

Emotions drive actions

The virtual pet solved a real user problem that simple numbers could not.

03

User stories save time

Always keeping our user, Arjun, in mind helped us make quick design choices without arguing.
